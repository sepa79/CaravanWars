# Historical World Tone

## Core Direction

CaravanWars is primarily a singleplayer MUD-like simulation with optional multiplayer-compatible architecture.

The world should not feel like a modern RPG society with medieval costumes. It should feel socially alien from a 2026 point of view.

The setting is early medieval and post-Roman inspired. Historical accuracy is not a trivia goal; it is a pressure system for travel, trade, fear, law, hierarchy, violence, rumor, and survival.

This is not dark fantasy. This is AD 750 as a design target: local power, fragile travel, personal dependency, poor information, uneven law, and survival under hierarchy.

## Social Hierarchy

Social rank is a core gameplay force.

The world is not socially equal:
- a powerful lord can behave like a local god,
- wealth and armed followers change what speech and violence cost,
- peasants, servants, slaves, captives, soldiers, women, foreigners, debtors, religious minorities, and outsiders may have very different practical protection,
- a soldier can be treated as expendable property of command,
- a poor traveler may be punished for actions that a protected noble can survive politically,
- law is mediated by patronage, force, witnesses, kin, church, oath, revenge, reputation, and practical reach.
- loyalty is local and personal: family, household, lord, caravan master, village, monastery, bishop, priest, shaman, war band, or patron.
- statehood is not a meaningful loyalty layer for ordinary people in the playable world.
- there is no modern nation, national identity, or civic state allegiance.
- power is experienced through persons and institutions that can feed, tax, bless, curse, judge, shelter, beat, conscript, or kill.
- if a guard eats because a merchant pays him, the merchant's good is his good until betrayal looks safer or more profitable.

This should affect:
- dialogue tone,
- intimidation,
- prices and bribes,
- access to roads, gates, ferries, markets, and shelter,
- witness behavior,
- rumor spread,
- willingness to report crimes,
- retaliation risk,
- faction and family memory.

## Brutality Without Spectacle

The world may contain cruelty, coercion, exploitation, raids, collective punishment, sexual violence, revenge, enslavement, torture, famine, disease, and legal inequality.

These should be represented primarily as:
- world facts,
- rumors,
- aftermath,
- reputation,
- fear,
- missing people,
- changed prices,
- abandoned routes,
- family or faction memory,
- consequences and retaliation.

The game should not rely on explicit shock scenes or eroticized cruelty. The goal is historical weight and social consequence, not spectacle.

## Human Baseline

The world should not assume modern liberal humanism as the default mental model.

Most people act through immediate survival, kin loyalty, patronage, fear, hunger, debt, habit, superstition, and remembered harm. "Good" often means good for me, my family, my lord, my village, my oath, or the hand that feeds me.

Common reasoning is shaped by:
- low literacy,
- poor information,
- malnutrition and exhaustion,
- alcohol,
- pain and disease,
- religious fear,
- rumor,
- local custom,
- short life expectancy,
- violence as ordinary political language.

This does not mean every person is stupid or cruel. It means the game should not expect modern civic morality, abstract rights, national identity, state patriotism, or universal empathy to be the default social operating system.

## Law And Blame

Law exists, but it is local, direct, uneven, and practical.

Common justice is closer to:
- catch the accused,
- force confession or testimony,
- punish visibly,
- compensate the powerful or injured party,
- restore fear and order,
- move on.

Punishments can be simple and brutal:
- fines or compensation,
- public beating,
- mutilation,
- hanging,
- exile,
- seizure of goods,
- collective punishment,
- hostage taking.

The hard part is not philosophical law. The hard part is finding someone who can be blamed and safely punished.

If the real offender is unknown, unreachable, protected, or too dangerous, the system may punish a convenient outsider, debtor, enemy, foreigner, servant, rival, religious minority, or socially weak person. The function of punishment is often order and deterrence, not truth.

This should affect gameplay:
- witnesses may lie to protect kin or patrons,
- accusations may follow status rather than evidence,
- strangers are useful scapegoats,
- powerful actors can redirect blame,
- a solved crime and an officially punished crime are not the same thing,
- rumor can become evidence if enough people need it to be true,
- exile or flight may be smarter than proving innocence.

## Player Agency

The player may control very different kinds of actors: merchant caravan, armed company, patrol, noble retinue, bandit band, city agent, or other field group.

Player intent can be kind, cowardly, honorable, pious, greedy, cruel, vulgar, deceptive, violent, or predatory. Whether a character can plausibly act on that intent depends on character rules, role, status, resources, witnesses, and consequences.

The LLM must not be the moral authority of the setting. Deterministic game systems decide what is allowed, what is possible, what is remembered, and what follows.

## No Heroic Immunity

There are no protected heroes who can safely rescue the oppressed because the story declares them righteous.

A heroic act against local power is usually a direct attack on someone's property, face, income, order, or authority. Without force, patronage, secrecy, witnesses, escape routes, payment, or political cover, the life expectancy after such an act may be measured in minutes.

Helping the weak is possible, but it is a strategic problem, not a genre guarantee.

It may require:
- armed backing,
- buying someone off,
- threatening a weaker link,
- appealing to a stronger patron,
- moving people before retaliation arrives,
- creating public shame,
- framing the act as lawful service,
- making revenge too expensive,
- accepting that someone else pays the price.

## Design Target

The target is not generic grimdark or dark fantasy.

It is closer to Sapkowski than to high-myth heroic fantasy. The world should not automatically reward good intentions or heroic restraint. Moral behavior may matter to conscience, reputation, faith, allies, and long-term memory, but it is not plot armor.

The target is a world where:
- travel is dangerous because protection is local,
- speech is dangerous because hierarchy matters,
- rumor is valuable because truth travels slowly,
- violence is political because power is uneven,
- mercy and honor are costly because the world does not assume them,
- oppression is systemic because the setting is not modern.

In blunt design terms: good means dead unless power, luck, witnesses, patronage, fear, or consequence protects it.

That does not mean cruelty always wins. It means survival depends on power, information, timing, witnesses, patronage, luck, reputation, and consequences rather than on the universe rewarding virtue.
